\begin{redbox}
\bhead{If you take one operational instruction from this document, take this one:} start
collecting \textbf{now}. Not at the hackathon. The single highest-value, lowest-effort
action available is a cron job on the cheapest VPS you can rent pulling ten routes from a free-tier
fare API into a SQLite file. Every day you delay costs a day of the only asset that cannot
be built under time pressure. A team with 45 days of real fare history and a mediocre
dashboard beats a team with a beautiful dashboard and four hours of data. Every time.

\section{The judge Q\&A drill}

Rehearse these out loud. Each answer is 30--45 seconds.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{``MoSPI already collects airfares online. What are you adding?''} \\
Daily instead of weekly; route-level instead of twelve online markets; five advance-purchase
windows instead of none; availability adjustment; component decomposition; a public API. The
delta table in \S1.2. Lead with the fact that you know CPI 2024 already does online airfare
collection --- that is the credibility moment.

\item \textbf{``You didn't do CAPTCHAs.''} \\
\S6.2.3, verbatim. The PS is internally contradictory; we resolved it the way a ministry must.

\item \textbf{``Why Jevons?''} \\
Because MoSPI uses it (FAQ Q.20); because it satisfies time reversal and transitivity;
because fares are lognormal. Show the three-formula slide.

\item \textbf{``How do you know your index is right?''} \\
We don't ``know''; we bound it. Standard errors by delta method, flight-block bootstrap CIs,
GEKS-Jevons and TPD as independent robustness variants with the spread published, and
agreement statistics against DGCA. \textbf{Never say ``accurate''; say ``bounded and
reproducible''.}

\item \textbf{``Is this legal?''} \\
Walk the four instruments (IT Act s.43/66, Copyright Act, Contract Act, DPDP), state that
\textbf{no personal data is collected} so DPDP does not attach, then show the provenance
ledger.

\item \textbf{``What happens when a site changes its layout?''} \\
Contract tests fail in CI. The source degrades to unavailable, the index suppresses affected
cells, coverage drops visibly. The system fails loudly and correctly, not silently and
wrongly.

\item \textbf{``How is this different from a flight price comparison site?''} \\
A comparison site answers ``what should I pay for this trip.'' A price index answers ``how has
the price of air travel changed.'' Different objects: one needs the cheapest current offer,
the other needs a constant-quality matched basket, weights, and a formula. That is why the
product key exists.

\item \textbf{``Can this scale beyond airfares?''} \\
Yes, and that is the real pitch to DIID. The Source Ladder, provenance ledger, cleaning
pipeline and index engine are item-agnostic. Airfare is the hardest case --- dynamic pricing,
perishable inventory, availability censoring. Hotels, cabs and the twelve online markets are
strictly easier. \textbf{This is the answer that turns a project into a platform, and it is
the strongest closing line available.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Closing assessment}

\begin{warnbox}
\bhead{An honest read on this problem statement.} It is a good PS for a team that can do
statistics and a bad one for a team that only writes scrapers --- which is why it is worth
picking if, and only if, at least one member is willing to own the index-number theory. The
scraping is the easy, commoditised half; every team will have it, and several will have more
coverage than you.

The differentiation is entirely in three places: \textbf{knowing that CPI 2024 already exists
and already collects airfares online}; \textbf{resolving the PS's internal contradiction
deliberately rather than ignoring it}; and \textbf{treating sold-out inventory as a price
signal rather than as missing data}. Those three things cost roughly a day of reading and
eleven hours of building, and no amount of dashboard polish substitutes for them.

Also: this PS is from MoSPI, which means the evaluator is likely a statistician, not a
software engineer. Optimise the pitch accordingly. A correct standard error will land better
than an animated map.
\end{warnbox}
