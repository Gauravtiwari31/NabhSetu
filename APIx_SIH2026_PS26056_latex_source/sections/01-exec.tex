\chapter{Executive Summary}

\section{The one-paragraph version}

APIx is a production-grade price statistics platform that collects domestic Indian
airfare quotes at high frequency from a legally defensible ladder of sources, cleans and
decomposes them into base fare, statutory levies, taxes and platform charges, and
compiles them into a family of published index numbers that are \emph{directly consumable
by MoSPI's Price Statistics Division and by the RBI's Monetary Policy Department}. The
index is constructed on the same mathematical machinery that MoSPI itself adopted for the
CPI 2024 series --- Jevons at the elementary level, Young/Modified Laspeyres above it ---
so that it can be plugged into COICOP sub-class 07.3.3 (\emph{Passenger transport by air})
without a methodological argument. Everything above that baseline --- daily frequency,
route granularity, lead-time resolution, availability adjustment, and a nowcast of the
Transport division --- is capability the official series does not currently have and
cannot cheaply acquire.

\section{The reframe: read the problem statement against reality}

\begin{redbox}
\bhead{The single most important thing in this document.}\par\vspace{4pt}
The PS background says CPI air fares are collected ``primarily through manual
price-collection from a limited set of outlets and ticketing offices.'' \textbf{That
statement describes the CPI 2012 series, which was retired on 12 February 2026.} Under
the CPI 2024 series --- released on 12 February 2026 with base 2024$=$100 --- MoSPI's own
FAQ states plainly that \emph{``Airfares are collected through well-known online
platforms''}, that online prices are collected \emph{weekly}, and that twelve online
markets have been added across twelve towns with population above 25 lakh.\footnote{MoSPI,
\emph{Frequently Asked Questions on CPI 2024 Series}, Annexure V to the CPI press release
of 12 February 2026, Q.14, Q.8 and Q.27.}
\end{redbox}

\noindent Most teams will not read the FAQ. They will build a scraper, put a Chart.js line
on a dashboard, and pitch it as ``solving'' manual collection. A MoSPI DIID evaluator
knows their own methodology. The moment a team says ``currently MoSPI collects airfares
manually,'' the evaluator stops listening.

So the honest framing --- and the one that wins --- is a \emph{delta} argument. What does
the CPI 2024 airfare item index still not do?

\section{The second reframe: the PS contains an internal contradiction}

\begin{warnbox}
The PS asks the system to handle \emph{``dynamic CAPTCHAs, anti-bot measures, IP
rotation, and session management''} and, in the same sentence, to remain \emph{``compliant
with the robots.txt and terms of service of source websites.''} These two clauses cannot
both be satisfied. Defeating a CAPTCHA is by definition circumventing a technical access
control; every major Indian OTA's terms of use prohibit automated access; and under
Section 43 read with Section 66 of the Information Technology Act, 2000, extracting data
from a computer resource without the owner's permission attracts a compensation-and-penalty
mechanism. A prototype that demos a CAPTCHA solver is demonstrating a liability, not a
capability.
\end{warnbox}

\noindent APIx resolves the contradiction by \textbf{inverting the priority}: compliance is
the hard constraint, coverage is the optimisation target. We implement a four-rung
\emph{Source Ladder} (Part IV, \S\ref{sec:ladder}) that exhausts licensed and
publicly-sanctioned channels before it ever touches an HTML page, records the provenance
and legal basis of every single quote in an append-only ledger, and treats a CAPTCHA
appearing on a page as a \emph{stop signal that is logged as a governance event}, not an
obstacle to route around. This is not a weaker system. For a Government of India
statistical product it is the only shippable one, and it is a USP no scraping-first team
can retrofit in 36 hours.

\section{What gets built}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\alph*.}]
\item \textbf{Collection engine} --- Python; \code{httpx}/\code{Scrapy} for API and static
sources, \code{Playwright} for JS-rendered pages that permit automated access, a
robots.txt-aware politeness governor, per-host token buckets, and a circuit breaker that
halts a host on the first hard block. Scheduled by APScheduler/Celery beat; containerised.

\item \textbf{Quote store} --- PostgreSQL 16 with TimescaleDB hypertables for the quote
time series, plus a Parquet cold tier queried by DuckDB. Star schema: one immutable
\code{fare\_quote} fact, six conformed dimensions, full slowly-changing history on
airport charges and tax rates.

\item \textbf{Index engine} --- a pure, side-effect-free Python module that takes a set of
cleaned quotes and a weight vector and emits index numbers. Jevons elementary aggregates;
Young/Modified Laspeyres aggregation; GEKS-Jevons and Time-Product-Dummy implementations
as robustness variants; bootstrap confidence intervals; deterministic and unit-tested
against worked examples from the IMF CPI Manual.

\item \textbf{Dashboard} --- React + Recharts. Daily/weekly/monthly APIx; sector heat map;
lead-time elasticity surface; fare-component stack; availability gauge; back-test overlay
against DGCA and CPI series.

\item \textbf{Public API} --- FastAPI, OpenAPI 3.1, JSON and SDMX-JSON output, API-key
auth, documented rate limits, and a \code{/provenance} endpoint that returns the audit
chain behind any published number.

\item \textbf{Evidence pack} --- 30 days of back-tested results, a reproducibility script
that regenerates every published figure from raw quotes, automated test suite with
coverage report, and a written methodology note in the format MoSPI uses for its own
discussion papers.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Brutal self-assessment}

\begin{warnbox}
\bhead{Where this project is genuinely hard, stated up front:}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The booking lead-time distribution for India is not public.} The weights
$\omega_\tau$ over advance-purchase windows are the single largest judgement call in the
index. We do not have a DGCA or MoSPI source for it. We handle this by making
$\omega_\tau$ an explicit, documented, user-tunable parameter and shipping a sensitivity
analysis; we do \emph{not} pretend it is measured. Judges will ask. Say this.
\item \textbf{Thirty days of back-test cannot be faked.} If collection starts on hackathon
day, there is no history. The mitigation is a two-track back-test: a live 30-day run
started as early as possible, plus a synthetic replay harness validated against published
DGCA monthly average-fare movements. Be honest about which chart is which.
\item \textbf{Coverage under the compliance constraint will be lower than a
scrape-everything team's.} We will have fewer carriers or fewer OTAs. The answer is not to
hide it; it is to show the \emph{coverage ledger}, quantify the resulting index variance,
and argue that a defensible 60\% is worth more to a ministry than an inadmissible 95\%.
\item \textbf{Airfare is a small CPI weight.} Transport is 8.796\% of combined CPI 2024;
air fare is a fraction of that. Do not oversell APIx as moving headline inflation. Sell it
as (a) methodological infrastructure MoSPI can reuse for hotels, cabs, and e-commerce, and
(b) a consumer-transparency and regulatory instrument for MoCA/DGCA.
\end{itemize}
\end{warnbox}
