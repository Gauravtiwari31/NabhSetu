\section{The ethical collection framework}
\label{sec:legal}

\subsection{The legal position, accurately stated}

India has \textbf{no statute that either permits or prohibits web scraping}. Liability is
assembled from four instruments:

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}L{4.6cm} X@{}}
\toprule
\bhead{Instrument} & \bhead{Exposure} \\
\midrule
\textbf{IT Act, 2000, s.43} & Compensation for a person who, \emph{without permission of the
owner}, accesses a computer resource or downloads, copies or extracts data from it. Also
covers disruption --- so excessive request volume is independently actionable. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
\textbf{IT Act, 2000, s.66} & Turns the s.43 conduct into a criminal offence where done
dishonestly or fraudulently. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
\textbf{Copyright Act, 1957} & Facts are not protected, but a \emph{compilation} can be, if
it satisfies the ``skill and judgment'' standard --- the test the Supreme Court applied in
\emph{Eastern Book Company v.\ D.B.\ Modak}. Fare tables plausibly qualify. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
\textbf{Indian Contract Act, 1872} & Terms of use are an enforceable contract. Breaching a
no-automated-access clause is a contract claim independent of the IT Act. \\
\addlinespace[2pt]
\textbf{DPDP Act, 2023} & Engages only if personal data is collected. APIx collects
\textbf{no personal data} --- fares are not personal data --- which removes this exposure
entirely, and saying so explicitly is a strong point. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\noindent Two international precedents worth knowing because judges may raise them:
\emph{hiQ Labs v.\ LinkedIn} (9th Cir.) held that scraping public pages is not
``unauthorised access'' under the US CFAA; \emph{Meta v.\ Bright Data} (2024) declined to
find breach for scraping \emph{logged-out} public pages, on the reasoning that terms bind
account holders. The emergent rule --- \textbf{logged-out public collection is defensible;
logged-in collection against accepted terms is not} --- is directly encoded in the APIx
product key, which mandates unauthenticated sessions.

\subsection{The Zero-Circumvention Policy}
\label{sec:ladder}

\begin{redbox}
\bhead{APIx will not, under any configuration:} solve, bypass or outsource a CAPTCHA;
rotate residential or datacentre IPs for the purpose of evading a block; create or use
accounts to obtain fares; spoof headers or fingerprints to impersonate a human after being
blocked; or continue requesting a host after a \code{429}, \code{403} or explicit block.

\bhead{APIx will:} read and cache \code{robots.txt} per host and honour it as binding
policy; declare a truthful, contactable User-Agent
(\code{APIx-Research/1.0 (+contact-url)}); enforce a per-host token bucket well below any
published crawl-delay; exponentially back off on any error; trip a circuit breaker on the
first hard block and \emph{escalate to a human}, not to a workaround; collect only
logged-out, and only from hosts whose terms do not prohibit it; and record the legal basis
of every single quote.
\end{redbox}

\subsubsection{The Politeness Governor}

A single middleware component every fetch passes through:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{robots.txt resolution.} Fetch, parse (\code{urllib.robotparser} or
\code{protego}), cache with TTL, snapshot to \code{dim\_source.robots\_snapshot\_id}. If the
target path is \code{Disallow}ed for our UA, the request is \textbf{never issued} and a
\code{ROBOTS\_BLOCKED} provenance record is written. The snapshot means we can prove, months
later, what the file said on the day we collected.
\item \textbf{Crawl-delay honouring.} If declared, use it. If not, default to a token bucket
of 1 request per 10 seconds per host with jitter, and a daily cap.
\item \textbf{Concurrency ceiling.} At most 1 in-flight request per host. Not 8, not 4. One.
\item \textbf{Backoff.} Exponential with full jitter on 5xx and network errors; immediate
stop on 403/429.
\item \textbf{Circuit breaker.} Three consecutive hard blocks on a host $\to$ open the
circuit for 24 h, emit a governance alert, and mark all affected cells as
\code{SOURCE\_UNAVAILABLE} so the index suppression logic --- not a silent gap --- handles it.
\item \textbf{Kill switch.} A single config flag disables any host instantly, and a
documented takedown contact is published in the API docs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{The Provenance Ledger}

Every fetch writes an append-only record whose hash chains to its predecessor:
\begin{equation}
h_n \;=\; \text{SHA256}\big(h_{n-1} \,\|\, \text{canonical}(\text{record}_n)\big)
\end{equation}
so the collection history is tamper-evident. The public \code{/provenance/\{index\_id\}}
endpoint returns, for any published number, the set of quotes behind it, the sources, the
robots directives in force, the rate limits applied, and the chain segment. \textbf{No
competing team will have this, and it is the artefact that makes the system deployable by a
ministry.}

\subsection{Why this is a stronger pitch than a CAPTCHA solver}

\begin{keybox}
Rehearse this exchange, because it will happen.\par\vspace{4pt}
\emph{Judge:} ``The problem statement asks you to handle CAPTCHAs. You haven't.''\par\vspace{3pt}
\emph{You:} ``Correct, and deliberately. The same paragraph also requires compliance with
terms of service and robots.txt, and those two requirements are mutually exclusive --- a
CAPTCHA is an access control, so defeating it is by definition unauthorised access under
Section 43 of the IT Act. We resolved the contradiction in the direction a Government
statistical product has to resolve it. Instead we built a four-rung source ladder that gets
the data through licensed APIs and permitted public endpoints, and a provenance ledger that
records the legal basis of every quote. Our coverage is $X\%$ of the basket; here is the
coverage ledger and here is the variance that coverage implies. A ministry can deploy this
on Monday. A CAPTCHA-solving prototype cannot be deployed at all.''
\end{keybox}

\section{Storage and compute}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hot:} PostgreSQL 16 $+$ TimescaleDB. Hypertable on \code{collected\_at\_utc},
1-day chunks, native compression after 7 days, continuous aggregates for daily/weekly
rollups. Indexes on $(\text{route\_sk}, \text{apw\_days}, \text{collected\_at\_utc})$ and on
$\kappa$.
\item \textbf{Cold:} Parquet on object storage, partitioned
\code{year=/month=/route=}, queried in place by DuckDB. This is what makes the 30-day
back-test re-runnable in seconds on a laptop.
\item \textbf{Transformations:} SQL models in dbt-style layering
(\code{staging} $\to$ \code{intermediate} $\to$ \code{marts}), each with tests. Keeps the
index logic reviewable by a statistician who does not read Python.
\item \textbf{Index engine:} a pure Python package, \code{apix\_index}, with \textbf{no I/O}.
Signature: \code{compute(quotes: DataFrame, weights: WeightSet, config: MethodConfig) ->
IndexResult}. Purity is what makes it unit-testable against IMF Manual worked examples and
what lets PSD run it on their own data without adopting our stack.
\item \textbf{Numerics:} NumPy/pandas; \code{statsmodels} for TPD and the elasticity
regression; \code{scikit-learn} for the Gaussian mixture; \code{statsmodels.tsa.x13} or
\code{STL} for seasonal adjustment.
\end{itemize}

\noindent \textbf{SDMX-JSON output is a deliberate credibility play.} SDMX is the
international standard for exchanging statistical data and metadata and is what
statistical agencies and central banks actually consume. Emitting a valid SDMX-JSON
structure --- even a minimal one --- says ``we know how official statistics moves between
institutions.'' Also emit an \code{application/json} shape for ordinary developers, and
attach an OpenAPI 3.1 spec so the docs are generated, not written.

\section{Security and governance}

\begin{itemize}
\item Secrets in environment/vault, never in the repo; pre-commit secret scanning.
\item API-key auth with per-key rate limits; read-only public tier.
\item No personal data collected, stored, or inferred --- state this in the README and in
the API docs. It removes DPDP Act exposure and is a genuine design property, not a
disclaimer.
\item Row-level immutability enforced by database grants, not just by convention.
\item Structured audit log of every configuration change to weights, basket, or method
version, with the operator identity.
\item Published takedown/contact channel, and a documented SLA for honouring a source's
request to stop.
\end{itemize}
